      🔍 Cite evidence

      📋 Florida B.E.S.T. (Grade 6):
      ELA.K12.EE.1.1 Cite evidence to explain and justify reasoning.
      ELA.6.R.3.2 Paraphrase content from grade-level texts.

      Don’t just say it. Prove it!

      A claim is what you think. Evidence is the proof from the text. Reasoning explains how the proof backs up your claim.

      💪 Claim: The author is patient.
      🔍 Evidence: The text says she sat still on the bank from sunrise until noon.
      💡 Reasoning: This shows she was willing to wait quietly for hours, and that is what patience means.
      • 📝 Paraphrase: say the evidence in your own words.
      • 💬 Short quote: copy a few exact words inside quotation marks.
      • 🗣️ Starters: “According to the text…”, “The author says…”, “This shows…”

      ✅ Strong evidence directly proves your claim. A random detail does not.

      Your teacher can write here during the lesson. You can write too if you want!

      📚 Read complex texts

      📋 Florida B.E.S.T. (Grade 6):
      ELA.K12.EE.2.1 Read and comprehend grade-level complex texts proficiently.
      ELA.6.V.1.3 Apply knowledge of context clues, figurative language, word relationships, reference materials, and/or background knowledge to determine the connotative and denotative meaning of words and phrases, appropriate to grade level.
      ELA.6.R.3.2 Paraphrase content from grade-level texts.

      Tough text? You have a toolbox!

      Hard texts have long sentences, big words, and lots of ideas. Good readers pause and ask, “Does this make sense?” If not, they use a tool.

      ✂️ Chunk itBreak a long sentence at commas and dashes. Read one piece at a time.
      🔁 RereadGo back and read it slowly again.
      🧩 Context cluesUse nearby words to figure out a new word.
      ❓ Ask questionsWho? What? Why?
      📝 SummarizeSay the main idea in your own words after each paragraph.
      🎨 Picture itMake a movie in your mind.

      Write a hard sentence, then slash it into chunks. Your teacher can write here during the lesson. You can write too if you want!

      💡 Make inferences

      📋 Florida B.E.S.T. (Grade 6):
      ELA.K12.EE.3.1 Make inferences to support comprehension.

      Read between the lines

      An inference is a smart conclusion about something the author does not say directly. It is not a wild guess. You build it from clues.

      🔎 Text clues
      what the author wrote
      +
      🧠 What I know
      from real life
      =
      💡 Inference
      what it probably means

      Example: “Sam checked the sky, packed an umbrella, and put on his boots.” Clues: umbrella and boots. I know: people use those when it rains. Inference: Sam thinks it will rain.

      🗣️ Starter: “I read that… and I know that… so I infer…”

      Your teacher can write here during the lesson. You can write too if you want!
